March 25, 2017

Early spring had just begun to stir on campus. With my camera in hand and nowhere in particular to go, I wandered beneath the trees, following the patches of sunlight as they drifted across the grass and pavement, hoping to collect a few ordinary corners of the day.

I didn’t think much of it at the time. It was only later, sitting at dorm and scrolling through the photographs, that I noticed how almost every frame held the same thing: sunlight scattered across the grass, spilling onto the pavement, breaking into little pieces through the leaves. Even the gentlest breeze seemed to have found its way into the pictures, caught in the trembling branches.

Looking through them, I felt as though I were remembering something I hadn’t known I wanted to keep—the warmth on my skin, the wind passing softly by, the unhurried afternoons that seemed so ordinary I never thought they would become memories.

I thought I had been photographing the campus. But maybe, all this time, I had simply been following the light.
